# Food vlog playbook

For the owner's planned food vlogs, in the cinematic, story-led style of Pakistani creators
such as Irfan Junejo. These notes come from Claude's general knowledge of that style and of
food-vlog craft; Claude has not watched specific videos for them (YouTube cannot be
downloaded in this cloud setup). When the owner says "I'm going to vlog at X", use this
to give: (1) the story angle, (2) what to say, (3) the shot list, (4) the edit plan.

## What makes this style work
- **A story, not a menu.** Each vlog has one question or feeling ("Is the most famous
  nihari in Karachi still worth the queue?", "A 40-year-old dhaba run by one family").
  The food is the payoff; people and place are the story.
- **Voice-over plus natural sound.** Calm, personal voice-over recorded after the shoot,
  over b-roll; natural sound (sizzle, chopping, crowd, the vendor's voice) left audible
  between lines. No shouting, no fake hype.
- **Cinematic b-roll.** Slow, steady shots, shallow depth of field, golden-hour or practical
  light, slow motion of steam, pours and flames, close textures. Wide establishing shots of
  the street or city.
- **Honest reactions.** First bite on camera, real reaction, short and specific ("the
  edges are crispy, the inside is soft").
- **Clean edit.** Minimal on-screen text (place name, dish name, price), warm film-like grade,
  soft music (lo-fi / acoustic / ambient) that drops under speech, J-cuts (sound before
  picture) between scenes.
- **Human moments.** Talk to the owner/cook: how long they've been here, their secret,
  their routine. One strong quote from them is gold.

## Structure (short 45–90 s reel, or 8–12 min YouTube)
1. **Hook (0–3 s):** the best-looking shot of the food or the strongest line.
   "Yeh nihari 1960 se ek hi deg mein ban rahi hai."
2. **Setup:** where we are, why we came (one sentence), establishing shot.
3. **Process:** how it's made: fire, deg, hands, dough, tandoor; the cook's quote.
4. **The bite:** reaction + 2–3 specific words about taste and texture.
5. **Detail:** price, timing, best item, tip ("raat 11 baje ke baad rush kam hota hai").
6. **Ending:** a feeling or verdict + CTA ("Aap ka favourite spot comment mein batayein").

## Standard shot list (shoot all of these at every place)
- Wide establishing: street/signboard, crowd, traffic (5–10 s, steady).
- Walk-in POV following you into the place.
- Signboard / menu / price board close-up.
- Cook at work: medium shot + extreme close-ups (hands, ladle, flame, dough stretch).
- Slow motion (60 fps): steam rising, oil sizzle, pour, cheese pull, bread puffing.
- Food hero shot: plate on the table, top-down + 45°, slowly push in.
- Your piece to camera (eye level, face lit by daylight or a lamp, not overhead tubelight).
- First-bite reaction (camera static, hold 3–4 s after the bite).
- Cook/owner interview: 2–3 questions, steady, their face lit.
- People enjoying (ask first), hands breaking bread, tea pouring.
- Transition shots: walking feet, car window, night lights, for J-cuts.
- Ending shot: you leaving / city at night / empty plate.

## Phone camera tips
- Lock exposure and focus (long-press on iPhone); shoot 4K 30 fps, slow-mo at 1080p 120/240.
- Clean the lens; keep the light behind the camera; avoid mixed lighting where possible.
- Hold each shot 5+ seconds, move slowly, use 0.5x for wides, 1x/2x for food.
- Record a lav mic or keep the phone close for voice; record 10 s of room tone at each place.
- Record voice-over at home later in a quiet room (blanket/closet = studio), phone 15 cm away.

## Questions to ask at every place
- "Kab se chal raha hai yeh?"  ·  "Sab se zyada kya bikta hai?"
- "Is ka secret kya hai?"  ·  "Din kab shuru hota hai aap ka?"
- "Price kya hai?"  ·  "Log kahan kahan se aate hain?"

## Edit plan (how Claude cuts it)
- Style: clean cinematic, warm grade, light grain, captions in the default style but
  fewer and smaller (voice-over vlog), place/dish names as small lower-third titles.
- Music: soft track ducked under voice; SFX only where natural (sizzle up, whoosh on
  transitions sparingly).
- Pacing: 1.5–3 s b-roll shots under voice-over, hold the bite reaction longer.
